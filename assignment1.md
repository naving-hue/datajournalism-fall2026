The main insight I gathered from Susan Carroll's article, "Nature ruled, man reacted. Her listings of specific stats paired with the insertion of various data visualization techniques seemed very credible and she included the source to these items as well. Quotes were used too so it seems unlikely that it was fabricated, although the "storytelling" format can sometimes make the situation seem less realistic. Nevertheless, she was diligent about source citing.

Statistics-wise, she used a ton making sure to include direct quotes, quantitative values, dates, times, locations, and names. The "engaging story" aspect was the only part I felt could have been stronger, but composing a story out of real-life events is also something I believe is more of a subjective quality. To me, the story was engaging and I was able to read it in one sitting. There *were* points where I could feel her pushing the idea of a "narrative" onto the reader but it didn't bother me because she did so in a respectful way without "tarnishing" the memory of such a serious situation. Finding the balance between drama and realism is what I mean by this. Creating a story that is both true and engaging without being misleading or disrespectful makes an idea story to me.
